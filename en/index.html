Academic Bio

From deep-learning flood forecasting to uncertainty-aware water modelling.

Xuan-Hien Le is a Lecturer at the Faculty of Water Resources Engineering, Thuyloi University, Vietnam. He received his Ph.D. from Kyungpook National University, South Korea, continued there as a postdoctoral researcher, and later worked at the Research and Development Initiative, Chuo University, Japan.

His research sits at the intersection of hydrology, hydraulics, remote sensing and data science. The publication trajectory spans streamflow and water-level forecasting, satellite and radar rainfall, flood inundation, scour and hydraulic structures, landslide susceptibility, Sentinel-1 SAR, uncertainty/XAI, and increasingly integrated physics–data approaches.

The objective is not simply to improve a metric, but to develop methods that are reliable, interpretable and useful for water-resources management and disaster-risk reduction.

Research Tips

Practical notes for water-science research

Short, method-focused lessons intended to improve the quality of conclusions rather than simply improve presentation.

Never validate with one metric alone

High NSE/R² can hide bias, peak timing errors or poor generalization. Evaluation should follow the intended use of the model.

Hybrid requires an architecture

Be explicit about where physics enters: constraints, features, residual correction or model coupling — and test the added value of each component.

Surrogates are useful only within known limits

Always test domain of validity and out-of-distribution cases. Speed is valuable only when confidence is calibrated.
