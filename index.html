About

Connecting physical understanding with data-driven intelligence.

My academic work has developed across Vietnam, South Korea and Japan, with a continuing focus on water-related hazards, hydrological prediction and the use of modern data methods to strengthen scientific understanding and decision support.

I am particularly interested in research problems where observations are incomplete, spatial variability is strong, and uncertainty matters. This includes flood-prone river basins, rainfall reconstruction, topographic uncertainty in inundation modelling, hydrological forecasting, remote sensing and the integration of machine learning with domain knowledge.

Research philosophy AI should complement — not replace — hydrological reasoning, physical modelling and scientific validation. My work therefore emphasizes performance, uncertainty, robustness and practical interpretability.

Featured research direction

Flood modelling under topographic uncertainty

Quantifying how DEM choice and elevation bias propagate into simulated water levels, inundation patterns and floodplain sensitivity — linking model uncertainty directly to flood-risk interpretation.

Featured research direction

Rainfall information for better hydrology

Improving rainfall inputs through radar–gauge merging, satellite bias correction and data-driven spatial reconstruction before they enter hydrological models.
